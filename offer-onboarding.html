Offer to Onboarding & Beyond

A thoughtful transition does not stop when a candidate accepts an offer. These two checklists help coordinate the offer, pre-start period and early placement experience.

Back to resources

Why this matters

The period between offer and starting a new role can bring excitement as well as uncertainty. Clear information, agreed expectations and appropriate check-ins help candidates feel prepared and supported. The recruiter remains a helpful point of contact while the client owns the employment and onboarding experience.

Checklist 1

Recruiter → Candidate

Offer & acceptance

  • Share feedback after each stage and explain the next step.
  • Before an offer, reconfirm interest, priorities, notice period, availability, compensation expectations and any other active processes.
  • Talk through the role title and responsibilities, salary, commission and wider package, proposed start date, expectations and any conditions.
  • Invite questions and explain required documents, approvals and next steps.

Pre-start contact

  • Check how resignation and handover are progressing and whether anything has changed.
  • Agree a thoughtful contact rhythm. For a four-week notice period, suggested touchpoints are early in the notice period and around one week before starting. Adapt this to the individual.
  • Confirm first-day arrangements, who to contact, where to go or how to log in, and anything they need to prepare.
  • Ask about concerns or support needs. Keep contact personal rather than making it feel like a checklist exercise.

Post-placement check-ins

Week 1

  • First impressions and onboarding.
  • Manager and team connection.
  • Role clarity, KPIs and immediate concerns.

Month 1

  • Does the role match what was discussed?
  • Training, support and workload.
  • Priorities and any gaps to address.

Month 3

  • Overall experience and progress.
  • Support and feedback.
  • Probation, where relevant.
Keep the relationship open: the purpose is to offer support and understand the early experience, not to manage the candidate's employment on the client's behalf.

Checklist 2

Client → Recruiter

Before the offer

  • Confirm title, responsibilities and any changes to the role.
  • Confirm base salary, full package, commission structure, targets and conditions.
  • Confirm proposed start date, approvals and any offer conditions.
  • Clarify KPIs, probation expectations, reporting line and key stakeholders.

Before the candidate starts

  • First-day arrangements: time, location or remote setup, contact person and what to bring.
  • First-week onboarding plan, introductions, manager and team support.
  • Equipment, systems, access and setup requirements.
  • First-month priorities, ramp-up expectations, targets and how feedback will be shared.
  • Probation structure and review points, where applicable.

When details are not confirmed

Clarify what is known, what is still being arranged and who owns the next update. Do not fill gaps with assumptions. If the client does not have a formal onboarding plan, be transparent and agree the practical essentials.

Suggested way to use the checklists: map the journey from verbal offer to the end of the first three months. Use the prompts to spot missing information, coordinate client and recruiter touchpoints, set reminders and check emotional readiness as well as logistics. Gather feedback and refine the approach as you use it.

The thinking behind it

It's not just the outcome. It's how people experience getting there.

Every message, conversation, interview and update shapes how a candidate feels about the process. Our aim is to create an experience that is thoughtful and consistent without losing the personal connection that matters.

Human first

Make space for real people, individual circumstances and honest conversations. Treat candidates with respect at every stage, whatever the outcome.

Clear and considered

Set expectations, explain what happens next and communicate proactively, especially when plans or timelines change.

Always learning

Use feedback, observation and practical measures to understand what helps, what gets in the way and where a small change could make a difference.

Our learning cycle

Listen, test, learn and refine.

Talent Experience is an ongoing improvement cycle. We start with an idea, shape it into something practical, test it in the real process and pay attention to what happens. Then we refine it and review what we've learned.

One connected experience, from first hello and beyond.

Learning from Phase 1

A resource is only useful if it reaches the person who needs it.

The Candidate Resource Hub has been trialled with candidates and refined through observation and feedback. It provides useful context before the first conversation, but the first review showed that access and introduction are just as important as the content itself.

What worked

When candidates accessed the Hub, it helped make information available earlier and gave them a place to understand Untapped, the process and what to expect.

What we learned

Some candidates did not see or open the Hub through the original sharing approach. We refined how it is introduced and shared so it is easier to notice and use.

Early operational signal

Screening calls were observed to be around 3.2 minutes shorter on average, at approximately 26.8 minutes, after the Hub was introduced. This is an early observation, not proof that the Hub alone caused the change.

Phase 3

Interview Experience

The interview resources are designed to make the process easier to understand for candidates and easier to guide for recruiters. The central guide should be tailored with the role, client and interview-specific details rather than sent as a generic script.

Candidate Interview Preparation Guide

Before the interview

  • Share the confirmed date, time, duration, format and meeting link or location.
  • Provide the job description or role overview, company website and LinkedIn details, and interviewer information where available.
  • Explain what the interview is likely to cover, any preparation or assessment required, and what happens afterwards.
  • Share feedback or next-step timing only where it has been confirmed.

Across the stages

  • Screening: help candidates understand the role, their motivations and practical considerations.
  • First client interview: explain who they will meet, the likely focus and how to prepare.
  • Further or technical stages: clarify the format, people involved and any task or assessment.
  • Afterwards: explain the feedback process and keep communication moving.

Keep in mind: preparation should reassure and inform, not overwhelm. Tailor it to the actual opportunity.

Internal Interview Experience Playbook

Use the playbook as a supportive guide, not a rigid script or a promise of a fixed timeline.

  • Map the interview stages, owners, assessments and likely questions so the recruiter can explain the process clearly.
  • Use candidate feedback and recruiter observations as early signals for what needs to be clearer.
  • Share the central preparation guide, then personalise it with role, client and interviewer information.
  • Check candidate understanding, confidence and practical readiness before each stage.
  • Follow up after interviews, clarify next steps and keep candidates updated when client feedback is delayed.

Requalification: respectfully revisit motivation, priorities, other active processes, notice period, availability, compensation expectations and deal-breakers at relevant points. Recheck after interviews, during longer pauses and before an offer. Explore counter-offer considerations without judgement.

Recruiter touchpoints: Screening · client interest confirmed · day before interview · interview day · immediately after interview · client follow-up · outcome or next round · before offer. Use these moments to check in, answer questions and close communication gaps.

Phases 4 & 5

Offer to Onboarding & Beyond

These connected checklists cover the journey from offer through pre-start and the first months in a new role. There are two perspectives to coordinate: the support the recruiter provides to the candidate, and the information the client provides to the recruiter. The goal is a smooth, well-understood transition, not to manage the candidate's employment on the client's behalf.

1. Recruiter → Candidate

Offer & acceptance

  • Share feedback after each stage and explain the next step.
  • Before an offer, reconfirm interest, priorities, notice period, availability, compensation expectations and any other active processes.
  • Talk through the role title, responsibilities, salary, commission and wider package, start date, expectations and any conditions.
  • Invite questions and explain required documents, approvals and next steps.

Pre-start contact

  • Check how the resignation and handover are progressing, and whether anything has changed.
  • Agree a thoughtful contact rhythm. For a four-week notice period, suggested touchpoints are early in the notice period and around one week before starting, adapted to the individual.
  • Confirm first-day arrangements, who to contact, where to go or how to log in, and anything they need to prepare.
  • Ask about concerns or support needs. Do not make contact feel like a checklist exercise.

Post-placement check-ins

  • Week 1: first impressions, onboarding, manager and team connection, role clarity, KPIs and any immediate concerns.
  • Month 1: whether the role matches what was discussed, training and support, workload, priorities and any gaps.
  • Month 3: overall experience, progress against expectations, ongoing support, feedback and probation where relevant.

2. Client → Recruiter

Before the offer

  • Confirm title, responsibilities and any changes to the role.
  • Confirm base salary, full package, commission structure, targets and conditions.
  • Confirm proposed start date, approvals and any offer conditions.
  • Clarify KPIs, probation expectations, reporting line and key stakeholders.

Before the candidate starts

  • First-day arrangements: time, location or remote setup, contact person and what to bring.
  • First-week onboarding plan, introductions, manager and team support.
  • Equipment, systems, access and any setup requirements.
  • First-month priorities, ramp-up expectations, targets and how feedback will be shared.
  • Probation structure and review points, where applicable.

When details are not confirmed

Clarify what is known, what is still being arranged and who owns the next update. Do not fill gaps with assumptions. If the client does not have a formal onboarding plan, be transparent and agree the practical essentials.

Suggested journey: map the experience from verbal offer to the end of the first three months. Use the checklists to spot missing information, coordinate client and recruiter touchpoints, set reminders and check emotional readiness as well as logistics. Pilot the approach in live placements, gather feedback and refine it.

Phase 6

Insights & Reporting

Keep measurement useful and proportionate. The aim is to turn activity and feedback into insight, not create reporting for its own sake.

Leading indicators

  • Clarity of information and next steps.
  • Communication and feeling kept informed.
  • Interview preparation and confidence.
  • Candidate support and process friction.
  • Engagement with the Candidate Resource Hub.

Lagging indicators

  • Overall candidate feedback and recurring themes.
  • Withdrawals and offer declines, considered in context.
  • Post-placement feedback and early experience.
  • Common questions, time spent and process efficiency.
  • Improvements made and what changed afterwards.

Use a small set of meaningful measures, combine numbers with real feedback, and report themes in aggregate. A trend is more useful than over-interpreting one response.

Phases 7 & 8

Scale and the full Talent Experience system

As practices are tested and refined, share what works across the wider team. Make resources easier to use, build consistent recruiter habits and connect candidate insight to everyday decisions. Over time, bring the journey, playbooks, reporting and continuous improvement into one joined-up system. Use AI and other tools where they genuinely make the experience clearer, more useful or more human, never simply for the sake of adding technology.

A shared effort

Good experiences are built together.

Talent Experience is shaped by everyday conversations and decisions. If something feels unclear, if a candidate keeps asking the same question, or if you spot a simple way to make a step better, that's useful learning.

Notice

Pay attention to candidate questions, feedback, moments of uncertainty and the parts of the process that work particularly well.

Share

Bring observations and ideas back to the team. Look for recurring themes rather than jumping to conclusions from one experience.

Improve

Try practical changes, see how they land and share what you learn so good practices can grow.

Why this matters

It's not just the outcome. It's how people experience getting there.

Every message, conversation, interview and update shapes how a candidate feels about the process. We want people to feel informed, prepared, respected and supported, whether they move forward or not.

Human first

Clear and considered

Always learning

What we're learning

Listen first. Look for patterns. Improve with care.

Early feedback gives us useful direction, but we stay mindful of sample size. We share themes and learning rather than treating a small number of responses as a complete picture.

Early candidate feedback

In an initial September 2026 feedback snapshot, five candidates responded. All five rated their overall experience 5 out of 5 and gave an NPS score of 10, resulting in an early NPS of +100.

Positive themes included warm recruiter interactions, clear communication, useful preparation material and constructive feedback.

5/5Overall experience rating
+100Early Net Promoter Score
2/5Mentioned waiting for client decisions as a stress point
One opportunity we're taking forward

Waiting for client decisions can create uncertainty. Where timelines are not confirmed, we can still acknowledge the wait and send a brief update so candidates know they haven't been forgotten.

This is a very small early sample, collected 1–9 September 2026. It is directional feedback, not representative research and not proof of cause and effect. We will continue gathering feedback and reviewing themes over time.

How we improve

Notice the details that make a difference.

We look at the experience from both sides: what candidates tell us, and what recruiters notice while guiding people through the process.

Candidate perspective

Clarity, preparation, communication, confidence, respect and how supported people feel at each stage.

Recruiter perspective

Repeated questions, missing information, process friction, delays and opportunities to make support more consistent.

Practical outcomes

Small changes we can test, review and keep when they genuinely make the experience better.

A shared effort

Good experiences are built together.

Talent Experience isn't a separate task or a one-off project. It is shaped by the everyday conversations and decisions we make as a team. If something feels unclear, if a candidate keeps asking the same question, or if you spot a simple way to make a step better, that's useful learning.

Share the observation. Try a thoughtful improvement. Notice what changes. Keep what works.

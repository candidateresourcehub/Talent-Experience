Phase 3 · Interview Experience

Helping candidates feel prepared, informed and supported.

Two complementary resources support a clear and considered interview journey: a guide for candidates and a practical playbook for recruiters.

Back to resources

Why the interview experience matters

Interviews can bring uncertainty, even for experienced candidates. Clear preparation, realistic expectations and thoughtful follow-up help people feel more confident and respected. A consistent approach also helps recruiters keep track of details without turning conversations into a script.

Candidate resource

Interview Preparation Guide

Before each interview, share the information that is confirmed and relevant to that particular role and client.

What the recruiter should share

  • Role overview or job description, plus the company website and LinkedIn page.
  • Interviewer names and roles, where available.
  • Confirmed date, time, duration, format and meeting link or location.
  • What the interview is likely to cover and any preparation or assessment required.
  • What happens next, including feedback timing only where confirmed.

Preparing across the stages

Screening conversation

  • Understand the role and candidate motivations.
  • Explore priorities, practical considerations and questions.
  • Explain the likely process and next steps.

First client interview

  • Explain who the candidate will meet and the likely focus.
  • Share relevant company and role context.
  • Encourage examples that demonstrate relevant experience.

Further or technical stages

  • Clarify format, people involved and expected duration.
  • Explain any task, technical discussion or assessment.
  • Share practical details early enough to prepare.

After the interview

  • Check how the candidate felt and whether they have questions.
  • Explain the feedback process and next step.
  • Keep them updated if client feedback is delayed.
A helpful reminder: preparation should reassure and inform, not overwhelm. Tailor the guidance to the actual opportunity and be clear about what is known versus what is still to be confirmed.

Internal resource

Interview Experience Playbook

Use this as a supportive guide, not a rigid script or a promise that every process will follow the same timeline.

Map the process

  • Know the likely stages, people involved, decision-makers and any assessments.
  • Clarify who owns each update and what the candidate can expect next.
  • Use the central candidate guide, then personalise it for the role, client and interviewer.

Requalify with care

Motivation and circumstances can change during a recruitment process. Revisit these topics respectfully at relevant points:

  • What matters most in the next role and whether priorities have changed.
  • Other active processes and how the candidate is comparing opportunities.
  • Notice period, availability, compensation expectations and practical constraints.
  • Deal-breakers, concerns or information that could affect a decision.
  • Counter-offer considerations, explored without judgement or pressure.

Useful moments to revisit alignment include after interviews, during a longer pause and before an offer.

Recruiter touchpoints

  1. At screening: understand motivation, priorities and practicalities.
  2. When client interest is confirmed: explain the next stage and share preparation.
  3. The day before: confirm logistics and answer last questions.
  4. On interview day: be available and check in where appropriate.
  5. After the interview: ask how it went and what questions remain.
  6. During client follow-up: own the update, even if there is no decision yet.
  7. At outcome or next round: communicate clearly and explain what happens next.
  8. Before offer: reconfirm interest, expectations and practical alignment.

Observe and improve

  • Notice repeated candidate questions, missing information and avoidable delays.
  • Ask whether candidates felt prepared, informed and supported.
  • Use candidate feedback and recruiter observations as early signals.
  • Review withdrawals or declined offers in context, without assuming every outcome was preventable.
  • Refine the guide and the way it is shared when patterns emerge.
The aim is a clear, supportive interview journey. Personal judgement still matters: adapt the level of detail and contact to the candidate and the process.
